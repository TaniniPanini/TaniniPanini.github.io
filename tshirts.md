It is a long-running Swiss tradition that for international Olympiads, we design a t-shirt for the Swiss delegation to wear at the event (typically, the opening ceremony). The spirit of a good t-shirt design is that it should be fun and capture three things:
- Something mathematical;
- Something that symbolises Switzerland;
- Something that symbolises the host country.

Since joining the SMO as an volunteer, I have designed our IMO team t-shirt every year (with the exception of 2021, where we did not make one - everyone forgot about it because of Covid.) I have also designed a couple of t-shirts for other competitions we attended. I do not use generative AI as part of my artistic process. 

Here is a gallery of all the designs I have made so far.

The front of the shirt is based on the [mutilated chessboard problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutilated_chessboard_problem). Two pandas are responsible for the mutilation of this particular chessboard. The chessboard squares are coloured so as to create the [Swiss cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Switzerland). The CH written on the board stands for both China and [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3166-2:CH). 

The back of the shirt pays homage to the card game of [Tichu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tichu). The team is drawn in the same style as eight of the Tichu cards, and the bearer of the shirt is calling "Grand Tichu", which is a bet one can make in the game that they will finish first. The colours of the cards represent the favourite topics of the contestants.

### EMO 2026 (Vilnius, Lithuania)

The front of the shirt parodies the [coat of arms of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Lithuania), where the knight has been replaced by [Globi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globi), who is bearing a compass and Swiss shield. The horse is now a geometric construction, but can still be seen in the background in the same style as images of constellations. The points defining the diagram are S, U, I, E, M, O (SUI is the FIFA/IOC three-letter code for Switzerland and EMO is the name of the competition). 

Our participation at EMO 2026 was made possible by a [crowdfunding initiative](https://wemakeit.com/projects/european-math-olympiad) and hence our backers are both listed on the front and back of the shirt. Not everyone gave their actual name; you might spot a clear case of [identity theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Berset), a [Hollywood star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbo_(2013_film)) and even [a classic drink from Slovenia](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bFos15_IExA).

### IMO 2025 (Sunshine Coast, Australia)

<figure style="width: 80%; margin: auto;">

    </div>
</figure>

The front of the shirt parodies the cover of the album [Back in Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_in_Black) by the Australian rock band [AC/DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC/DC). Instead of AC/DC, the letters [AM-GM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AM%E2%80%93GM_inequality) are written instead as a reference to the mathematical inequality. Below, a [visual proof of the inequality](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:AM_GM_inequality_visual_proof.svg) is given, with parts of the [Sydney Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Opera_House) and the [Matterhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matterhorn) drawn inside the sectors of the circle, as well as Australia in the lower hemisphere. The caption is "BAC in black", which is a play on the album title and literally indicates that the angle BAC is coloured in black. 

The back of the shirt has a tracklist that has mathematical plays on the names of several famous AC/DC songs. The mathematical terms are references to the favourite topics of the contestants, ordered by [their performance at the 2025 Team Selection Test](https://imosuisse.github.io/exams/selection-2025/). The duration of the songs refer to their birth month (minutes) and score on the TST (seconds). The contestant for Liechtenstein is listed as a bonus track.

<figure style="width: 80%; margin: auto;">
    <div style="display: flex; gap: 10px;">

The front of the shirt depicts [Pingu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingu) in a literal bath, with two soap bottles in a Swiss and Liechtensteiner design and a paper boat made out of the [Union Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Jack). 

The back shows [Heidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidi) reading a sign for [Aquae Sulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquae_Sulis), the Roman settlement that predates modern-day Bath. Heidi indicates that she got lost looking for [Aquae Helveticae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquae_Helveticae), the Roman settlement that predates modern-day [Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden,_Switzerland). Incidentically, "Baden" literally means baths, and that years' team leader David Rusch is from Baden.

### BMO 2024 (Varna, Bulgaria)

<figure style="width: 80%; margin: auto;">
    <div style="display: flex; gap: 10px;">
        <img src="assets/images/bmo2024front.png" style="width: 50%; object-fit: cover;">
        <img src="assets/images/bmo2024back.png" style="width: 50%; object-fit: cover;">

</figure>

The front of the shirt is an original tiling problem about the [Swiss cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Switzerland), which is exactly to scale and has 204 unit squares. It asks if the cross can be tiled with 4 x 1 rectangles, possibly rotated.

The back of the shirt provides a proof by colouring that this is impossible. The colours used are white, green and red for the [flag of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Bulgaria) and the letters B, M, O (the name of the competition) are inscribed in the squares.

The front of the shirt parodies the [coat of arms of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Russia), but with a [decidely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrolean_hat) [alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lederhosen) [double-headed eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-headed_eagle). The [imperial crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_crown_of_Russia) is replaced by the [Matterhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matterhorn), the shield now bears the [Swiss cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Switzerland), and the scepter and orb have been replaced with a [fondue fork](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourchette_%C3%A0_fondue) and a bar of chocolate. There is a cowbell attached to the tail of the eagle. 

The back of the shirt depicts the members of the team as [Matroyshka dolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matryoshka_doll).
